🔗 How the Two Lenses Work Together

The dominance lens tracks volume — which categories have the most companies and where that share is heading. The emergence lens tracks velocity — which small categories are accelerating and might become the next big thing. Neither alone tells the full story.

✓ Converging Signals

  • Hard Tech — both lenses see growth: emergence detects 4.30× momentum, dominance shows +1.5pp trend. High-confidence signal.
  • Hardware & Manufacturing — emergence flags 5–8× multipliers, dominance confirms upward trajectory. Physical-world tech is the real deal.
  • Developer Tools declining — emergence detects the biggest negative mover, dominance confirms the share halved (20%→10%). A category dissolving into verticals.
  • Defense & Security — emergence sees 3.5× and 1.9× growth from small bases. Dominance shows these are still niche by volume but trending up. Worth watching.

△ Complementary Views

  • Emergence catches early signals dominance can't see — Browser Agents (7.80×), Drug Discovery (3.69×), Autonomous Drones (4.15×). These are too small to move the share needle today, but emergence detects them before they show up in dominance rankings.
  • Dominance catches broad shifts emergence can't see — Industrials (5%→23% of batch) is a massive macro trend, but it's a broad industry tag, not a niche. Fintech's recovery is invisible to emergence because it's a comeback, not a new formation.
  • RL is an example of the lenses diverging — emergence sees 4.17× momentum (big multiplier on tiny base), dominance sees it flat. Emergence is right that something is happening; dominance is right that it's not yet material by volume.
  • Robotics shows why you need both — emergence says Manufacturing & Robotics is still accelerating; dominance predicts mean-reversion from an S26 spike. The truth is probably somewhere between: real growth masked by batch-to-batch variance.

💡 Reading the Signals Together

When both lenses agree (Hard Tech, Hardware, Manufacturing) → high conviction. These categories are growing AND gaining share. The signal is strong across both velocity and volume.

When emergence is ahead of dominance (Browser Agents, Drug Discovery, Autonomous Drones) → early signal. These are the categories to watch. They're too small to matter by volume today, but if the emergence signal sustains, they'll show up in dominance next cycle.

When dominance shows something emergence doesn't (Industrials, Fintech recovery) → macro shift. These are broad structural changes in what types of companies YC funds, not niche breakouts.

The highest-conviction read for S27: AI remains dominant by volume, Industrials is the macro story, and the most interesting emerging signals are in Hard Tech, Coding Agents, and Defense — where both lenses point in the same direction.

🔬 Methodology

Data: Cambrian Signal (YC formation data, version yc-2026-08-14-semantic-v7) + YC OSS API (6,179 companies).

Preprocessing: Company counts per category per batch, normalized to share of batch total (batch sizes 94–398). Emergence categories use keyword matching on company descriptions where YC tags don't exist.

Model: Time series forecasting, zero-shot. 2-step ahead point forecast + quantile outputs (10th–90th percentile).

Emergence data: Niche category signals informed by Cambrian Signal's formation momentum analysis. Their whisper rankings detect × multiplier on share change; we validate those signals against per-batch time series trends.
