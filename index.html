Framework

How the Two Lenses Work Together

The dominance lens tracks volume — which categories have the most companies and where that share is heading. The emergence lens tracks velocity — which small categories are accelerating and might become the next big thing. Neither alone tells the full story.

✓ Converging Signals

  • Hard Tech — both lenses see growth: emergence detects 4.30× momentum, dominance shows +1.5pp trend. High conviction.
  • Hardware & Manufacturing — emergence flags 5–8× multipliers, dominance confirms upward trajectory. Physical-world tech is the real deal.
  • Developer Tools declining — emergence detects the biggest negative mover, dominance confirms the share halved (20%→10%).
  • Defense & Security — emergence sees 3.5× and 1.9× growth from small bases. Dominance shows niche by volume but trending up.

△ Complementary Views

  • Emergence catches early signals — Browser Agents (7.80×), Drug Discovery (3.69×), Autonomous Drones (4.15×). Too small for dominance but worth watching.
  • Dominance catches broad shifts — Industrials (5%→23%) is a massive macro trend invisible to emergence because it's a broad tag, not a niche.
  • RL shows the lenses diverging — emergence sees 4.17× momentum, dominance sees it flat. Something is happening but not yet material by volume.
  • Robotics needs both lenses — emergence says still accelerating; dominance predicts mean-reversion. The truth is probably in between.

Methodology

How This Was Built

Data: Cambrian Signal (YC formation data, version yc-2026-08-14-semantic-v7) + YC OSS API (6,179 companies).

Preprocessing: Company counts per category per batch, normalized to share of batch total (batch sizes 94–398). Emergence categories use keyword matching on company descriptions where YC tags don't exist.

Model: Time series forecasting, zero-shot. 2-step ahead point forecast + quantile outputs (10th–90th percentile).

Emergence data: Niche category signals informed by Cambrian Signal's formation momentum analysis. Their whisper rankings detect × multiplier on share change; we validate those signals against per-batch time series trends.
